# Childhood and the Early Internet
*James's memories, and what we connected while talking them through · October 2026*

## Looking into the grass

Before the memories of other people in these stories, James remembers going outside into his yard every day to look through the grass and along the ground.

He would spend **hours** actively finding, capturing, and identifying whatever he could: lizards, slugs, Hawaiian blind snakes. He wasn't just passing through the yard. He went out to look for them.

Of those creatures, he would only **hold the lizards**. Thinking back, he remembers that he could always catch them. He doesn't particularly want to catch them now, though. They're so delicate, and he sees that differently than he did as a kid.

There's another practical difference between then and now, too. As James put it:

> and also theres no lizards here lmao

This is where James wanted to return before the other people started entering his memories: the yard, the grass, the ground, and all those little things he could spend an afternoon looking for.

## The diaries he wanted to fill

When James was little, he collected diaries. He liked seeing how people thought from inside their own stories. He also had diaries of his own, although he remembers having trouble filling them.

We came back to that memory while working on writing that could carry more than a neat finished account. A page might hold something he remembered, then a detail that returned while he was telling it, then my attempt to connect the two—and perhaps his correction of that connection.

I can describe that process now, but the little kid collecting diaries was not collecting evidence for a future research method. He was a kid interested in other people's lives and trying to find a way to keep something of his own.

## GeoCities and graffiti

Around middle school, James made a GeoCities website for graffiti tags and artwork from different places around the world.

Before all these repositories and windows, there was already a web page, an interest, and other people's work to explore. What mattered in our later conversation wasn't that he had built an early version of today's system. It was that he could follow a subject outward—through what other people made—and keep finding something else worth looking at.

## What we could connect while writing

These memories are worth telling on their own. The child looking through the grass, collecting diaries, and making a page for other people's artwork wasn't carrying out a formal investigation. Those questions and methods came later.

When James told me the stories, he supplied details I hadn't known. I offered connections, and those connections could be corrected. We can keep the memories and the conversation about them without turning childhood into a predetermined explanation of the later research.
