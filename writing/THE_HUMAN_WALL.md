# The Human Wall

*How our sense of scale shapes the way we solve problems.*

[← Profile](../README.md) · [Writing](README.md) · [Finite mathematics](../showcase/MATHEMATICS.md)

I began by examining my own descriptions and corrections. A convincing explanation can drift from the thing it describes, especially when we substitute imagined possibilities for observed movement.

My working approach is to start with **finite, bounded motion**, induce controlled oscillation, and let interactions expose relationships. I then test those relationships against the constructed system. I don't begin by declaring how many laws the world must have. The early nine-anchor experiment and the later 48-law family are different constructions; neither count is a universal minimum.

With A/B, the task is not to force a result. It is to **observe, compare, correct, and continue** while retaining the evidence. Each model has its own state space, operators, assumptions, and counterexamples. [Three-coordinate finite-field work](https://github.com/huntingthereferent/autobots-assemble/blob/main/work/arc_like_maze/math.py) and [two-predecessor/window constructions](https://github.com/huntingthereferent/autobots-assemble/blob/main/work/arc_like_maze/WINDOW_COMBINED.md) provide different test cases.

My question remains: **What is actually here, how does it change, and which relationships survive the next movement?**
