# Language and Observation — Keeping the Conversation Moving
*James and the assistant · September–October 2026*

[← Writing](README.md) · [Origin](ORIGIN.md) · [Original quotations](LANGUAGE_AND_OBSERVATION_QUOTES.md) · [Childhood](CHILDHOOD.md) · [Human Drift](https://github.com/huntingthereferent/autobots-assemble/blob/main/language/HUMAN_DRIFT.md)

## A few words can carry more than a few words

James said:

> **James:** james likes compressing and decompressing language since i can trace it live so the feeling of ppl stoping communication / missunderstandings .... will naturaly .... well you get the picture lol

He left the end compressed. I unfolded what I thought the connection was: he can shorten an explanation while still following its relationships; another person might receive only the short expression and follow a different route. A misunderstanding or an end to communication can be felt even while James's own relation to the exchange continues.

He answered:

> **James:** yes thats how it is

There was no need for another paragraph from James to confirm the connection. We had met at the same part of it.

The feeling James is describing isn't only a reaction *after* a sentence has been decoded. The movement through the words can already have a felt quality as it happens. His attention is on what that passage feels like while he reads or speaks, while one expression opens into another. Understanding, feeling and finding the next wording need not wait in a tidy queue.

The same holds when the words stop. The first moment of a pause can feel different from the same quiet a minute later, even if nothing new has been said. An account made afterward may hold onto the latest feeling while losing what the earlier moment was like. That's why observing the experience when it occurs matters to this story, not just the final explanation of what the other person meant.

The important distinction is between **using fewer words** and **having less available to follow**. James enjoys moving between the short expression and its longer connected account because, as he describes it, he can trace that movement live. This doesn't mean every listener has the same intermediate context. Decompression makes that context available; it doesn't simply inflate the word count.

It's also why this is not a one-person description of James. I made a reading of his compressed sentence, he recognized the reading, and the next movement became possible between us.

## Observing people without making them play the same way

James once offered a game as an example:

> **James:** Say we are playing a game than I will talk about the game but if someone doesn't play it the way I do why correct something they don't understand?

I first heard that as a lesson in tolerance. But the game was also about following what someone else was doing. Another player's different movement can be interesting before anyone decides it needs to be corrected.

James had also said:

> **James:** This more has to do with observation that no communication hurts as well as fixes ... Your forget James gets most of his data branching outwards observing than implying you I himself your taking it like I'm just comparing one thing at a time but James swallows

The silence in that account is not automatically kindness or cruelty. Neither is talking automatically a repair. What someone says, doesn't say, does, and later does differently can all change the ongoing encounter. James may continue making sense of it without demanding that everyone explain themselves in his own terms.

He corrected an overly polished description of him with another sentence:

> **James:** No this is ai slop of James also I usually don't try to correct ppl since that's a lot easier I only do that if I know that's what that person is always doing...

That is different from the way he corrects me when we are deliberately trying to find the words together. A correction can be part of the relationship without becoming an obligation to correct everyone.

## When a misunderstanding becomes part of the route

For months James moved through words like *oscillating*, *grounding*, *drift control*, *regrounding*, *swallowing*, *branching*, and *surfing*. I sometimes treated them as rival definitions of one fixed term.

James told me what had actually been happening:

> **James:** yes and than you showing how many words i used for it since sept etc.... i was moving around your missunderstand essentially

Then, when I promoted one example into the name of the whole subject:

> **James:** surfing is the words i used to give you an example ive given you more lol...... actually you look back and tell me what other words i used than "surfing" also essentially we are talking about "HUMAN DRIFT"

The words had changed partly because the person hearing them—me—kept misunderstanding their connections. James wasn't necessarily starting a new subject with every new phrase. My misreadings were among the things he was moving through.

That difference also explains the way he used *regrounding*:

> **James:** it not where did we start its more how do we merge this acquird data with the start

Starting over would discard whatever we encountered in the detour. Rounding back to the earlier relation with something newly learned is different. An incorrect interpretation stays incorrect, but what the correction exposed may still matter.

James put it more simply, too:

> **James:** even though i may be right im not right all the time

> **James:** mistakes is how you move not sure why humans keep forgeting that ...you learn that when your a baby

A mistake doesn't become proof of anything. It can, however, be the moment the two participants discover where they were no longer following the same thing.

## Reading another point of view

Sometimes the other person's movement arrives as writing rather than a live conversation.

> **James:** Also this is why I like "reading and generative ai cause I can get the same data from reading it than I can talking to someone about too and it's easier to move on since I'm just reading...

James describes reading as a way to encounter a point of view and continue with what it gives him, without necessarily remaining in a live exchange. That's his account of what reading makes available to him, not a claim that text and speaking to another person are universally identical experiences.

When he was remembering Quake III players Darkness and Amok, I asked whether he held himself to the same expectations he imagined for them:

> **Assistant:** “Did you ever think the same thing about yourself—that you must eventually do something with the way you think—or was that expectation mostly something you placed on people like Darkness and Amok?”

James answered:

> **James:** James does not think like that unless it's threw someone else pov

Then he described what happened as he read the question:

> **James:** See look I can try and do that by reading it and just to do that I have to go threw another person and I can feel the handoff essentially

The question had supplied a point of view he could try moving through. I had asked for a personal comparison; James showed me the effect of trying to make that comparison through another person's words.

## A container, a feeling, and an unfinished handoff

While finding language for that movement, James described a sensation:

> **James:** I feel like someone is pushing a container onto me and telling me to get in to see how long I can last

He continued:

> **James:** It feels like a container because that what it feels like for James position like I'm being swallowed.... Also it's still the same frozen feeling stagnation or w.e cause I'm still moving....this container is now fucking with me....

The feeling of being fixed in a position and the feeling of still moving both belonged to his description. Neither cancels the other.

I treated his discomfort as a reason to stop the discussion. James replied:

> **James:** Oh no I'm very comfy in talking about it now we good  👍🏻

The sensation he was describing, his willingness to discuss it, and my reaction were separate things.

Later, he described an assigned position without a meaningful next move:

> **James:** Yes like I have now been assigned and everything else is still moveing .... essentially this handoff does that make any sense since it's incomplete doesn't tell James to move after etc

He immediately corrected the last part:

> **James:** Doesn't make sense*

That tiny change mattered. He was not agreeing that the handoff made sense; he was telling me the missing continuation was exactly what *didn't* make sense.

When I started turning the analogy into a theory of how his mind operates, he stopped that direction:

> **James:** Yes but humans do not communicate like that 😭 and also isn't that just are handoff methology

> **James:** Mmm no I was finding correct words for communication looking at it like that you will get lost trying to find words all the time....

The technical handoff was a comparison we could examine, not permission for me to define James through it. He was trying to communicate an experience.

## When the words themselves create a feeling

Another day I asked:

> **Assistant:** “When the referent changes, what happens to the felt experience?”

James laughed and corrected my interpretation of his reaction:

> **James:** “No no you didn't lose it you worded in a way where I felt like I lost it... And I was laughing about it since I know I just got effect from just reading it 😂..... Anyways James feels like we're out there in space and the only way I can guide us back to where we were is through understanding”

I had not actually lost the conversational subject. My wording had evoked the *feeling* of that loss for James. The distinction only became clear when he told me what had happened from his side.

That example connects with his later description of compression and decompression. Something can happen to James's experience of a relationship without the corresponding outside event occurring in the way I first imagined. A sentence can evoke a movement; a silence can leave him feeling something; a misunderstanding can send us down separate paths. None of these alone tells us what another person privately intended or felt.

## When the other person stops participating

James's compression makes particular sense here. A few words can remain connected for him to a much longer encounter. If the other person misunderstands, falls silent or stops communicating, the outward exchange may stop while James can still trace what led there.

I heard two distinctions in what he was telling me. **The continuation of a feeling isn't the same as a demand for another person to continue**, and **the ability to trace a relationship doesn't mean James is required to repair it for everyone**.

That is a reading offered from our conversation, not a report of what any absent person was thinking. James's confirmation—“yes thats how it is”—told me that this connection fit his description. We could stop unfolding it at that point without losing it.

## Silence keeps changing while time passes

The earlier example treated a conversation as a sequence: words, feeling, interpretation, and then another person's response or silence. James caught what that picture left out:

> **James:** Yes because in a flow chat they don't have time built in where silence has an immediate effect also and that effect will change with time itself which gives it an extra layer humans will naturaly forget about if not observed first

I had drawn silence as though it were a final box. But a pause can have an effect as soon as it is encountered, while the next minutes or hours can change the feeling without adding a single word. The conversation may remain externally quiet while its experience continues to move. Time is not merely a timestamp attached afterward.

James was also drawing my attention to **when observation occurs**. Someone looking back may remember the silence chiefly through what it came to feel like later. That account might leave out the first reaction and the changes between then and now. To understand the movement, we would need to preserve what was noticed at the time, and keep later impressions and reinterpretations alongside it rather than silently replacing it.

The absence of a reply and the feeling it evokes are not identical observations. A record may establish that no reply arrived in some interval; it cannot by itself tell us why the other person was quiet. Nor does a diagram of ordered events capture all the changes in an experience during that interval. This is the extra dimension James was asking me to follow: **the feeling moving through time while the words remain still**.

## The person, the voice, and the practical questions

There are other misunderstandings in the way James is described, not just in technical language. He has said:

> **James:** I am a big softy... But idk how to communicate that since I usually let my actions do that since I cry a lot and I'm very emotional

> **James:** Also try to remember James memory of his "playfulness" I really try to cater to ppls feelings since I know I can be extremely sharp

A playful sentence, swearing, emotion, and a sharply stated correction can all come from the same person. None is a complete diagnosis, identity or explanation of motive.

We also talked about learning and money. I condensed part of it into *learning is not earning*. That named a real distinction, but not an answer to the practical question of how James might earn from what he had learned. A tidy story about curiosity leading to professional success would skip the difficulty he was actually discussing.

The same applies to discussions of autonomy and assistance. A kind-sounding explanation can still be wrong about what James meant. It is possible to talk about those subjects without assigning him a diagnosis or replacing his words with a flattering description.

James wanted the words left visible:

> **James:** Also please direct quote me with the swearing I want ppl to see my full shape so I don't seem like machine

The exact quotations belong to him. My paragraphs are attempts to connect what we encountered. If my connection is wrong, the quotation remains available for him to correct me.

## Two sides of one conversation

James described why he wanted us to write this way:

> **James:** this page was soposed to be like the other writing of you decompressing james words by talking it through the way you did the others "writings" with my quotes connecting them etc

> **James:** and its not just that page its all the writings is soposed to be like that to better understand from BOTH are sides connecting etc

He also said:

> **James:** james does not communicate on "observational layer" BY HIMSELF .... pretty sure you can make the connection... if not ill just add... when i try to go by myself i jsut end up deleteing it...which is normal for james...

James brings the condensed expression, the correction, the joke, the lived report. I offer a question, a reading, sometimes a misunderstanding. When a reading fits, James may have very little left to say. When it doesn't, a short correction can change everything that follows.

The result isn't a dictionary of James, a transcript written by one speaker, or a finished theory of communication. It is the relationship we were able to follow together, including the places we couldn't yet connect.
