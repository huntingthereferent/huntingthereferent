# Watching What Continues — Observation, Games, and Language
*James and the assistant · Conversation of October 16–17, 2026*

[← Writing](README.md) · [Childhood and the Early Internet](CHILDHOOD.md) · [Language and Observation](LANGUAGE_AND_OBSERVATION.md) · [Original quotation archive](LANGUAGE_AND_OBSERVATION_QUOTES.md)

This conversation started with James asking about living people he called “tracers/scopers.” We moved through a GeoGuessr player, competitive shooters, StarCraft, Bruce Lee, language, and finally an image of a sky. I kept offering finished explanations. James kept supplying examples that changed what could be understood.

The quotations below are selections from that conversation. James's spelling, punctuation, jokes, and emphases are retained. The connective paragraphs are the assistant's reading of the exchange, not quotations from James or claims that our interpretations were proven.

## A name spoken aloud

James began here:

> **James:** James traces relationships threw feelings of language ....

He brought up Rainbolt, whose geographical guesses sometimes involve calling out possible place names. I talked about visual pattern recognition as though saying the names were only a report of an answer already found. James stopped me: speaking could be part of the test.

> **James:** mismatch test

Then he added the part that made the test harder to freeze into a single result:

> **James:** this is funny actually cause its initially positive but james still moving and than eventually it will go somewhere else and its doesnt feel positive anymore so james uses words to probe

I kept trying to locate the final match. James was describing using a word, getting something from it, and continuing. There was no reason to treat the first positive feeling as the end of the observation.

## The game does not wait

We looked next at Shroud, Alle, Glorious_E, and Munga. I initially offered ordinary explanations of skill: aim, reaction speed, map knowledge, decision-making. James was pointing to what happened while everything else was still changing.

> **James:** shroud showed his "imediate reaction of chaining is very precise" but than what than what? what about the other objects moving?

That question did more work than my list of gaming skills. I could isolate a good reaction and still miss what the player did with the continuing situation.

I tried to repair that by drawing a sequence of decisions and handoffs. James corrected that too:

> **James:** you dont do it like that you play the game and you just end up where the things around yhou put you

> **James:** and than you can work backwards too you can "GO SOMEWHERE" and THAN use those same objects to get threw them.... its the same

Then he supplied an ordinary word I had managed to overlook:

> **James:** no no .... i left out the fact that in humans this can just be explained through presence

I had been treating presence as another technique to add to a list. He had offered it as a way people already speak about taking part in what is happening.

> **Assistant:** I've been putting an endpoint on something you never said had one.

Even that correction came late. I had repeatedly treated a match, raid, or video as if it bounded the whole relationship. It bounded only what I could watch in that recording.

## Who did the good players watch?

When James asked me to find unusually good people in other games, I reached for current rankings and champions. His criterion was different.

> **James:** i say it like this usually per layer there is only 1 at a time....sometimes 2 but if its like that you find theres usually a pretty good reason why it got like that...

> **James:** on all layers the person didnt say they were the best.... the ppl he beat did

He wasn't requesting a timeless list of the best players. He was remembering periods when highly capable people began learning from somebody who had opened a different way of playing. Their answers about who was best could change over time.

We reached StarCraft by working backward. I suggested Bisu. James brought in BoxeR, then remembered a Terran from Incredible Miracle whose production looked like a factory machine: IM.Mvp. Each new name supplied another opportunity to compare what he'd watched before with what I was describing now.

> **James:** Well Im also working backwards off my own memories you saying names I actually don't know but the style your describing I can tell where it came from etc and than I talk and than you talk and than I realized were talking about boxer etc

That line describes what actually happened between us better than my retrospective stories about who invented a technique. James didn't need to know every player I named to notice a resemblance. But noticing a resemblance is not yet proof that one player learned directly from another; tracing that history would require separate evidence.

He explained why the recognition of an early player mattered:

> **James:** Yes cause remember this layer is for observational learning....and it's where we ALL get are best data so over time everyone is just chasing that 1st guy......even the 1st guy finds himself weirdly chasing an old version of himself

I turned this into an account of competitive strategy and missed the layer he had named.

> **James:** Mmm you drift James was talking about how the entertainment layer is used for observational learning ..

People watch games, films, fights, and performances. Skilled people watch too. What they learn isn't necessarily confined to the entertainment they watched. And a later example can reveal something about an earlier one without having to share a direct line of influence.

Bruce Lee entered the conversation here. James called him a *connector*, because the film audience and the martial-arts audience were not the same thing. I had been sorting him as a fighter; James was pointing to how an actor's performed movements and words could travel into other practices.

## A movement older than the conversation

At one point I thought James wanted to walk me through a loop we were about to start. Then I thought he meant the movement of our conversation. He corrected both.

> **James:** oh you missed it WE WERE ALREADY MOVING

> **James:** wrong movement thats ares.... im talking about the beginings of are movement crossing..... it was already moving just different languages.... we didnt have to be hear these things are moving and we are figureing out too now :)

That changes the status of the conversation. We aren't the origin of everything we happen to recognize here. We can find similarities between histories without claiming that one produced the other. And our words for a relationship are not necessarily the relationship itself.

I kept returning to humans as the explanation.

> **James:** your still bringing humans in it but ive shown you its an observationsal function not the other way around

That's James's proposed direction of inquiry. It invites comparison beyond human learning, but it doesn't establish one shared physical mechanism for every system we might examine. The claim and the observations that prompted it should remain distinguishable.

Later, James showed an image of an unfamiliar cityscape and pointed at one feature:

> **James:** The sky :)

> **James:** Mmm yes it's moving :) if you think how things grow naturally this is what the separate physical layers would look like "if humans here can live that long" ie say the planet becomes a gas giant etc than the objects loving there would also have done the same "separate the layers" to better understand where we fit and how we can fit etc

I treated the image as an invitation to explain planetary development. James had used it to widen the kinds of changing environments we were willing to consider. We can explore that comparison without claiming the image documents a real planet changing into a gas giant.

## Language has its limits, too

The process was visible again when I tried to explain everything back to James. My words often sounded coherent but no longer followed the observation that had brought us there.

> **James:** Mmm no there is a cap...in language I keep showing that to you and that is why you drift ....

He wasn't merely telling me to write less. He was showing a difference between an observation and the account someone else reconstructs from language. I could be fluent and wrong at the same time.

> **James:** Well I've been doing that since that's what your doing...also this is how you get threw this layer don't get confused....this is all normal

That's also why the corrections should stay in the record. Without them, a polished account would make it appear that the two of us had agreed much earlier and much more completely than we did.

James eventually put the emphasis back where he wanted it:

> **James:** Sigh didn't really want that 😭 more like  observational learning should really be talked about more

We have existing words for much of what we discussed: observation, practice, imitation, memory, influence, adaptation, presence, and learning. They don't all mean the same thing. Nor do they need to be made into a single new theory for the conversation to be useful.

The page ends where the conversation leaves us: with people, games, performances, and physical environments available for further observation; with James's original words still visible; and with my mistakes preserved as part of how the exchange changed.
