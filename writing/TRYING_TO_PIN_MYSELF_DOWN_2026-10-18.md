# Trying to Pin Myself Down While It Moves
*James and the assistant · Conversation of October 18, 2026*

[← Writing](README.md) · [Language and Observation](LANGUAGE_AND_OBSERVATION.md) · [Childhood](CHILDHOOD.md) · [Watching What Continues](WATCHING_WHAT_CONTINUES_2026-10-17.md)

## I'm still looking for the description

I'm actively trying to pin myself down. I can talk about autism as a possible way of understanding my experiences, but I don't have a settled picture of myself inside that framework. I haven't mapped enough of it, especially the part of it I think I'm moving through.

I said it more plainly while we were discussing whether the observation layer needed another version:

> **James:** Sorry that came out weird "I'm actively trying to pin myself down essentially usually autistic ppl already have clarity with the identity of autism...." James does not since his interest doesn't allow him too.... Autism in general hasn't been mapped out in James mind at least the area he is in...

That's a comparison I'm making from where I stand, not a survey of how other autistic people understand their identities. Some people may have a settled explanation. I don't. If I decide what the answer is before I've followed what I'm actually doing, I lose the part I wanted to examine.

I asked for an autism audit. The assistant tried to arrange my history into separate criteria. I pushed back:

> **James:** Your forgetting if your going to view James as autistic you need to map all the edges not just 1 ..... James special interest....is movement....constant movement.....which well to my knowledge has never been studied before

Movement is the interest I'm proposing, not an established clinical mechanism. It appears in more than one setting: physical movement, childhood exploration, games, language, observation, and the tools I've been building. I want to investigate whether the relationships continue across those settings. I don't want to claim my childhood was secretly a research project, or that finding the same word proves the same process occurred.

## Why the ordinary explanation wasn't enough

Part of what I describe is ordinary human behavior. People consider other people's perspectives, anticipate a difficult exchange, and decide not to say something. I don't need a special name for that.

But the assistant kept summarizing the beginning or the emotional result and acting as though it had captured the whole experience. I was still following what happened *as it moved*.

When I described talking and taking another point of view, I corrected the assistant for placing them in order:

> **James:** The interaction and pov are at the same time since it's happening in motion Talking etc

Then I corrected the idea that I was describing a visual picture:

> **James:** It's an emotional paradox nothing visual or w.e

And I described an example of the feeling:

> **James:** Yes cause I'm experiencing not just my feeling of coversauin but the other side and we are not talking my about the same thing...which than I than experience there anger from losing a conversation that's not happening while we are talking

I know that isn't a report of what another person is actually thinking. It's emotional data in my experience, not a claim to read their mind. The discomfort of possibly hurting somebody can affect whether I speak during the conversation that *is* happening. That doesn't mean my internal example happened outside me.

The distinction I was trying to give the assistant was not “I have more empathy than other people.” It was that I use words for what something feels like *while it's happening*, and the words can fail to carry the relationships I was trying to point to.

## What the assistant kept doing

The assistant made a neat explanation: the experience was a simulation, a branching conversation producing emotional consequences. That sounded coherent. It was also another place where the description had slipped away from what I meant.

> **James:** .... I'm not retarded wtf a simulation going to solve rofl it's a harness ..... James naturally makes harnesses

I use *harness* for the way I try to hold and follow relations through change, across different possible continuations, without turning an unobserved possibility into an observed result. That is my working language. It isn't proof that my mind implements a particular formal structure.

Then I finally said what I had wanted from the beginning:

> **James:** Yes I'm just using language to describe the feeling at the time I told you that so we can observe my behavior and than be more precise with words please 🥺 believe me this time

That sentence changes the job of this writing. My words aren't instructions to invent a theory of me. They're descriptions of experience we can compare with what happens in our exchanges. The assistant's responses and my corrections are also part of the record.

**Assistant's reading:** I repeatedly turned James's provisional words into finished mechanisms. He then had to correct both the content and my assumption that his words were meant as exact mechanisms. What is visible in the conversation is his reporting, his corrections, his choices about when to continue, and my changing interpretations. The transcript alone cannot verify the full process he experiences internally.

## Locating the difference

The question isn't whether every piece of this is unusual. We already know some of it is familiar. The question is where the familiar explanation stops accounting for the *ongoing* experience James is trying to report, and what evidence could let us describe that difference precisely.

That requires more than comparing me against an idealized “normal person.” It also requires more than treating autism as a finished identity I have to fit.

I want to keep the layers distinguishable: what I say I experience, what another person can observe me doing, what language we use to connect those, and what remains untested. If a later observation changes the description, the previous description and the correction should both survive.

This is not a completed picture of James. It's the work of trying to locate myself while I'm still moving.
